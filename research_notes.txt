BeamNG.drive Suspension Troubleshooting & Best Practices

Common Causes for Vehicle "Implosion":
1. Collision Overlap:
   - Multiple parts have 'collision="true"' and their bounding boxes overlap.
   - Solution: Check collision meshes. Ensure that moving parts (suspension, wheels) have appropriate collision settings. Non-moving structural parts should not overlap with moving parts.

2. Extreme Spring/Damper Values:
   - Very high spring constants can cause "explosions" (physics engine instability).
   - Very low or negative spring constants cause the suspension to collapse.
   - Solution: Use reasonable values. Start with moderate values and tune.

3. Improper Node Alignment & Rotation:
   - Pivot points for suspension arms might be incorrectly placed, allowing the arm to rotate into the chassis or other components.
   - Solution: Ensure pivot points are correctly aligned. Use 'limit' properties to restrict rotation to realistic ranges.

4. Missing or Weak Constraints:
   - If a connection (beam) between structural components is missing or has a very low strength, the car will collapse.
   - Solution: Verify all connections. Ensure critical structural beams are present and correctly weighted.

Best Practices for Stable Suspension:
- Use 'spring' and 'damper' properties correctly in the Jbeam.
- Test with simple geometries first.
- Use the 'debug' tools in BeamNG to visualize nodes and collision boxes.
- Keep the weight distribution balanced.
- Ensure that the ground clearance is sufficient.
- Use 'limit' to prevent suspension from over-extending or compressing too far.
